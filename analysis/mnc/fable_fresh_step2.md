kitchen bin, the lid-release button on a kettle, the spout button on a flask/drink bottle, the on/off switch on a
wireless keyboard, the push-button of a pump-pot, and pushing the batteries (not the cover) into a keyboard/remote.

Conflicts and how I resolve them:

* **Battery-gadget hints vs. the thud and ring.** Sarge rated "batteries into a compartment" hot and the mouse
  on/off switch very very close, but a hand-held mouse or remote gives a 1 to 3 ms clean click with no 62 Hz thud and
  no 0.3 s ring, and the owner's A/B of battery-compartment clips found no match. I trust the audio: the object is
  larger, hollow, and either sitting on a bench/desk or is itself the thudding body. The battery hints most likely
  mean the answer contains "switch", "button" or "pushing ... in", or that the object is a battery-powered thing
  that lives on a desk or bench (keyboard, scale, timer, lantern) rather than in the hand.
* **Toaster "very close" vs. toaster rejected five times.** Toaster is the wrong object; "pushing down the
  button/lever" is the right action. The sound is not a toaster pop (the "pops up" guesses were flat no's), so the
  second event is not a toaster carriage.
* **"Cheap microwave" / "tinny" vs. a Q-120 ring.** Not a conflict: thin stainless or hard plastic parts on cheap
  appliances (kettle lids, flask spouts, bin lids, keyboard plates) ring exactly like this.
* **"Echo-y" vs. the dry recording.** The room is dry; "echo-y" must be the object's own hollow body (a bin, kettle,
  flask, keyboard shell). That is consistent with the 62 Hz Q 5 mode being the object rather than the bench.

## 4. What to record or search next for the top five

Search-library terms (1 to 2 words): `bin lid`, `pedal bin button`, `kettle lid`, `lid release`, `thermos button`,
`flask lid`, `bottle lid click`, `keyboard switch`, `slide switch`, `battery insert`, `remote batteries`, `pump pot`,
`airpot`, `latching button`, `push switch`, `stick blender`.

Record each candidate with a phone (a) lying on the same bench/desk and (b) held 30 cm away, then run the scripts in
`analysis/mnc/fable_fresh/` (01, 03, 04, 06) on the WAV. A match must show all of:

1. **Two events 100 to 200 ms apart, the second 2 to 5 dB louder**, the first a 10 to 15 ms crescendo (press that
   seats), the second an abrupt 2 ms onset with 10 to 15 ms of chatter (sprung part hitting its stop). A single
   press/release of a latching switch (candidate 4, 8, 16) gives this only if the release is spring-driven; a
   lid-release button (1, 2, 3) gives it naturally.
2. **A soft low thud peaking 20 to 30 ms after each click**, ringing at 50 to 80 Hz with T60 0.1 to 0.2 s. Bin,
   kettle, flask and pump-pot bodies on a bench should give it with the phone on the bench; it must survive lifting the
   phone if the body itself is the resonator (bin, flask) and may vanish if it is the bench (keyboard, batteries).
3. **A 750 to 850 Hz ring, T60 0.25 to 0.45 s, present only after the second event.** Tap-test the lid, spout, plate
   or spring on its own to locate it; for the bin it is the thin lid, for the kettle the lid or body, for the flask
   the steel body, for the keyboard the plate.
4. **The same 1 to 3 kHz mode comb after both events** (one hard-plastic housing struck twice).
5. **Nothing between the events**: >2 kHz level between the two clicks 35 to 45 dB below the peaks (no scrape,
   hiss, whir, liquid). This rules out the SodaStream, soap pump and salad spinner quickly.

Priority order for the owner: (1) press-to-open kitchen bin lid, (2) kettle lid-release button, (3) flask/drink
bottle spout button, (4) wireless keyboard on/off switch with the keyboard on the desk, (5) pump-pot button; then
(6) batteries pushed into a remote/keyboard resting on a bench, since Sarge's warmest words sit there even though the
audio argues against it.

On-air phrasing to try first, in order: "pushing the button on the kitchen bin to open the lid"; "pushing the button
on the kettle to open the lid"; "pushing the button on the thermos to open the lid"; "pushing the on/off switch on
the wireless keyboard"; "pushing the button down on the pump-pot".
